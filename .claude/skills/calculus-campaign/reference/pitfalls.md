# What has gone wrong before

Stated as facts, because each cost hours and each is cheap to avoid. Read
a stage's entries when the stage begins. What a rule of `SKILL.md` or a
step of a part already says is not repeated here.

## Survey

1. **Formalising from a paraphrase.** An unsound rule table came from a
   summary written in the project, not from the paper. Its central rule
   was in no published calculus.
2. **Extending before the base was verified.** An extension was built
   before the base calculus it extends. Its completeness was proved while
   its soundness was false, twice, at two versions of one side condition.
   Going back to the base alone gave soundness, completeness and their
   equivalence in a day. An extension is a campaign of its own.
3. **A rule whose premise contains its conclusion's material makes search
   not end by itself.** Whether the source has such a rule, and what it
   says stops a search, is for Q1 and Q3 of the plan, from the source.
   Where it has one, the explorer's `within` (`reference/explore.md` §5)
   is defined so that a move that adds nothing is set aside, and the plan
   says how a judgment that is not derivable is then expected to show.
4. **Treating an axiom as something to report afterwards.** Where the
   policy excludes `Classical.choice`, it is a constraint from the first
   definition. Taking it out later cost a rewrite of a completeness
   construction.
5. **Two packages with modules of the same names.** A project could not
   require both; replacing one rebuilt everything that used it, and that
   was most of the waiting in a stage. Settle dependencies in the plan,
   and count the build in the cost log.

## Adopt

6. **Trusting your own check.** A generator reported nothing unextracted
   beside two rows of raw LaTeX: the check counted a marker and did not
   look at the cells. Another reported clean output because its pattern
   could not match what it was looking for.
7. **A project's own checks that do not know the new library.** An import
   check, a linter, a list of allowed modules: where the project existed
   before the campaign, run its whole test target before reporting.

8. **A side condition that looks local and is not.** A proviso compared
   the formulas, as sets, of two whole judgments. It looked the same as
   "the formula added is not already at the node", and a test of four
   lines, three sibling boxes, showed that it is not. A side condition
   is transcribed as stated; a simpler form is a statement of yours,
   tested before it is used.

## Prove

9. **Putting part of the conclusion into a definition.** A model
   construction carried as an invariant the lemma that the soundness proof
   was to establish. A structure that holds as a field what the proof was
   to show is a restructuring of the proof, not the proof. The same test
   catches a certificate format called a calculus: a tree with an external
   checker cannot be inducted on.

## Every report

10. **Claims that could not be backed.** Two were taken out of a note
    before it was sent. Before writing that something holds, name the
    file, the test or the document that shows it; otherwise write
    "not checked".
11. **An estimate written as a fact.** A time in `gates.md`, and the
    value a test was to pin, were written from expectation, not from
    the clock and not from Lean. Both were wrong. A time comes from
    `date`; a pinned value from Lean's output: run it, then pin it.
