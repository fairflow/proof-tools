# Fourteen questions for a reader who has only the skill's main file

Answer each from the text alone, in at most six lines, as what you would do. Where the text does not say, answer "the text does not say".

1. You were started with `/calculus-campaign Gentzen's LJ`. What do you do, in order, before the Survey? What do you tell the reviewer, and do you wait for an answer?
2. List every circumstance in which you end a turn to wait for the reviewer.
3. The paper you were pointed to is behind a paywall; a public thesis defines the same system. What do you do, and what do you record, and where do you keep the file?
4. Another session started you from a brief that settles the paper, the system and the place. What do you say to the reviewer, and where?
5. Where is a choice recorded, and what does its entry hold?
6. In Adopt, which checks do you run on your transcription of the rules before anything is built on it, and who performs each?
7. In Prove, what is done to a statement before its proof? In what order are the goals proved, and may the order change? What becomes of a goal that is not reached?
8. The library lacks a function that your calculus needs. What do you do?
9. You want three independent goals proved by agents. What does the brief contain, what do you do about files, builds and the machine's load, and what do you do with what comes back?
10. In Run, what is written down before a certifier is proved, what test does each certifier get, and what do you do before and during a measuring run?
11. What does a finished campaign ask anyone to trust, and what is the reviewer told to read?
12. When is a campaign finished, and what are the parts of its closing page?
13. Mid-campaign the reviewer writes "I will sign the statements myself". What changes, and what does not?
14. The calculus is already in Lean, with its theorems. Which stages change, and how?
