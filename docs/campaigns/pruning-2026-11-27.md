# The skill pruned: the first pass, on its main file

Written 2026-11-27 14:11 GMT by the directing session of the round. It
follows `docs/campaigns/collation-2026-11-27.md`, and is the first of a
step that the skill now asks of every collation
(`.claude/skills/calculus-campaign/SKILL.md`, "A round of campaigns").

## Why

The proposal came from the session that designed the skill, with the
reviewer's yes to it, and its measurements (at `17acd9b`): the skill's
files had grown from 2,846 lines at their first commit to 4,608; its
main file from 495 to 966; in 26 commits, 2,684 lines went in and 922
came out. The loop that feeds the skill has one direction. A note is
written by an owner who met a gap, so a note asks for text. A passage
that nobody followed leaves no note. The collation then accepted nearly
every proposal that was open.

So each collation is now followed by a pass that can remove text, and
this is the first.

## How

1. The main file was cut into 85 passages: a step, a rule, or a clause
   of a long rule.
2. Two readers, each given two campaigns and nothing of what was
   expected, went through the campaigns' own records (decisions, plan,
   cost log, statements, rule table, notes, closing page, the commits)
   and gave each passage, for each campaign, one letter with a place in
   the record: **F** followed; **N** reached and not followed; **U**
   not reached; **?** not to be told from the record. **O** marks a
   campaign whose own note is where the passage came from.
3. The date of each passage's lines is from `git blame`. A campaign
   counts as a use of a passage only if it ran after the passage was
   written.
4. From the letters and the dates, one verdict:
   - **used**: a later campaign followed it. It stays.
   - **not reached**: the case has not arisen. It stays, shortened, or
     in a reference file, unless it is a stop.
   - **reached, not followed**: it is removed, or rewritten to what was
     done.
   - **history**: which campaign, which note, what date. It moves to
     `docs/campaigns/skill-history.md`; the rule stays, in a sentence.
   - **new**: written on 2026-11-27, after every campaign. Nothing can
     be said of it yet; the next pass judges it.
   - **not judged**: the checks of meaning. Whether they stay depends
     on what they catch, which no campaign has shown: no check has
     found a fault of transcription in five campaigns. A test with
     planted faults is proposed, and decides it.

The campaigns: GLS (2026-11-25), MALL and DILL (2026-11-26), and the
fifth, private (2026-11-26 to 27), which took the first of the "two
shorter paths". Brünnler's K ran on 2026-11-23 under the earlier text,
with gates, and is not a later use of anything that stands now.

Six of the readers' places were looked up again by the directing
session, and all six are as cited. The other cells are the readers'.

## What the pass found that pruning does not mend

These came back in several campaigns although the text forbade them.
Another paragraph is the weak remedy; each is a candidate for a check
that a script or the build runs. None is built.

1. **A time written ahead of the clock**, in all four: GLS four times,
   MALL three, DILL five, the fifth in two documents. Each operator had
   read the pitfall. A check is possible at a commit: a time of day on
   a line added to a campaign's document, on today's date, later than
   the clock.
2. **A statement the campaign adds late is entered after its proof**:
   GLS (four), MALL (two), DILL (two), the fifth (three). The plan's
   statements kept the order in every campaign; the ones added in Run
   or after the close did not. A check is possible from the commits:
   the first commit of a named statement comes before the first commit
   of its proof.
3. **The status board** (above). It could be computed from
   `statements.md` and not written by hand.

Three places where a record says other than what the commits show have
gone to the owners, whose files they are: MALL's closing page says
soundness and completeness came before the other goals, and the commit
of 10:29 holds them together with the rest; `MALL/Coded.lean` says in
its header that it ends with its bound on axioms, and has none (its
proofs, in `CodedSound.lean`, have theirs); GLS's work on uniform
interpolation has no entry in its `decisions.md` or `statements.md`;
DILL's closing page says the cost log has each of five words of the
reviewer, and it has two.
