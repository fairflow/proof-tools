and with them `Valid Γ ↔ Nonempty (K Γ)` (`valid_iff_derivable`), the
proofs as data (`complete : Valid Γ → K Γ`, `cutElim : Kcut Γ → K Γ`),
and S3, the six rules in the figure's shape (`statements.md`).

What each rests on, so that it can be weighed:

* **Soundness.** By induction on the proof. A rule sound at a node is
  sound in a context by induction on the path (`NSeq.deep₂`): the
  source's Lemma 2.8, proved on the models and not through a Hilbert
  system. That the model is two-valued is used at the axiom and at
  `◇k_c` only. `cut` needs no such hypothesis.
* **The search.** At the root: the rules `∧`, `∨`, `□` while one
  applies; then the axiom; then, for each box `[Δ]`, the search of
  `Δ, B₁, …, Bₖ` for the root's diamonds `◇B₁, …, ◇Bₖ`. It ends by
  structural recursion on a bound of the sequent's weight. A proof of
  one box gives a proof of the whole by `◇k_c` `k` times and
  `Fam.lift`. If every box is refuted, the refuting trees are the
  children of a tree that refutes the whole.
* **Completeness.** The search's proof; a refutation would be a state of
  a two-valued model where a valid sequent is not true.
* **Cut elimination.** Soundness of K + cut, then completeness. It is
  "cut is admissible". The function `cutElim` gives the search's proof:
  it does not transform the proof it is given.
