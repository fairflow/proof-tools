How the proofs go, so that they can be weighed:

* **One theorem for weakening and exchange** (`Fam.sub`). `Γ` embeds in
  `Γ'` if the formulas of each node are among those of its image,
  counted, and distinct boxes go to distinct boxes. A proof of `Γ` then
  gives a proof of `Γ'`, no deeper: every rule instance is carried to
  the image of its node.
* **One theorem for the four inversions** (`Fam.invert`). The same, with
  some occurrences of the rule's formula replaced, at any nodes, by what
  the premise has for it. Where a rule acts on an occurrence that is
  replaced, its premise is already what is wanted.
* **One theorem for contraction** (`Fam.hom`). `Γ` maps into `Γ'` if
  each formula of a node is in the image, and each box goes to some box,
  two possibly to one. By induction on the depth. For a rule on `X`: the
  hypothesis gives the premise with `X` kept; `X` is inverted there;
  the hypothesis again makes the two copies of the premise's formulas
  one.
* **The finite model** is the refuting tree of the search cut at the
  modal depth of `Γ_F`: a state is a level and a tree of that level.

The source's proofs go by cases on where one thing lies inside another.
