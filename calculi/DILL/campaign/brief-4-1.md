## The task

Write, for dual intuitionistic linear logic (`calculi/DILL/`, a lake
package; run every command from that folder):

1. **`tutorial.md`** (in `calculi/DILL/`): the tutorial page in the
   pinned format, as `../MALL/tutorial.md` is for the neighbouring
   calculus: read that page whole first, and
   `../../.claude/skills/calculus-campaign/reference/explore.md` §11
   ("A tutorial page": every displayed statement is a pin printed by
   Lean, never typed; statuses are computed; a REFUTED statement is a
   paraphrase pin; links name a commit). The script is
   `../../scripts/tutorial_pins.py` (`python3 -I ../../scripts/tutorial_pins.py write tutorial.md`,

   `<!-- lean-pins: module DILL.TutorialPins; import DILL …; open DILL -->`.
2. **`DILL/TutorialPins.lean`**: generated by the script's `write`; do
   not write it by hand.
3. **`DILL/TutorialTest.lean`** (a module of the library; namespace

   every claim a comment makes is pinned in `DILL/TutorialTest.lean`.
6. **`campaign/users-guide.md`**: how a goal reads, the twenty moves with
   their rules and what each asks, the lines of the tactic, the
   messages, the two notation traps (`(!A)`, `(A ⊕ B)`), the sign `⨾`,
   and which certificate answers what. Model:
   `../MALL/campaign/users-guide.md`.

**The reader.** A logician who knows sequent calculi and has met linear
logic, and who wants (a) to use this two-zone calculus by hand, (b) to
know exactly what has been proved about it in Lean and how each proof
goes, without reading the Lean. Explain each theorem's proof in a
paragraph or two of ordinary mathematics (the measure of the cut
elimination; why the unrestricted cut needs its own induction; the
canonical model of pairs (set, multiset) and why its idempotents are the
unrestricted contexts; why saturation refutes with no semantics; why
decidability stops at `!`), and say that this prose is a reading, not
checked. One short section places the calculus: the zone of valid
hypotheses as the typing context of unrestricted variables in a linear
type system; `!A ⊸ B` as the ordinary function type; Caires and
Pfenning's reading of the same sequents as session types (CONCUR 2010);
cite, do not expand. Standard technical language only; no invented
terms; statements as displayed formulas. Do not use the word "honest"
for a caveat or a limit, and avoid the dash set off by spaces (use a
comma, a colon or a full stop).

**The page's parts** (adapt MALL's): 0 before you start (how to build,
how to type a move, the two notation traps); 1 the logic and where the
calculus comes from (Chang, Chaudhuri, Pfenning 2003, Figure 2; Barber
1996; the misprint in the figure's ⊕R rules, divergence W1 of the rule
table); 2 the calculus, rule by rule, with a worked derivation or two
(`· ; !A ⟹ !A ⊗ !A`; `· ; !A, !(A ⊸ B) ⟹ !B`); 3 exercises A
(derivations) and B (resources: what fails without weakening and
contraction in the linear zone, what copy gives back); 4 the structural
properties (exchange; weakening and contraction of the unrestricted
zone, PROVED; of the linear zone, REFUTED, each with its pair of
sequents); 5 cut: the two cuts, the admissibility of each, cut
elimination as a function, and again as a proposition through the
semantics; the inversions (CCP Lemma 6) and the two deduction theorems;
the six rules that are not invertible, each REFUTED with its sequent;
identity expansion; the subformula property; consistency and the
disjunction property; 6 the one-zone calculus and the translation
(`Γ ; Δ ⟹ C` exactly when `!Γ, Δ ⊢ C`); 7 the phase semantics, soundness
and completeness without cut, and that validity does not depend on the
order of the hypotheses (`valid_perm`); 8 what runs: the search with a
budget and its tree of moves; the decision for sequents without `!` and
with an empty unrestricted zone; saturated sets as certificates against
a derivation; why there is no complete answer with `!` (undecidability:
Lincoln, Mitchell, Scedrov, Shankar 1992, Corollary 3.8, cited, not
proved here); exercises on each; 9 what rests on what; 10 solutions.
About twenty-five graded exercises in all, each solved in
`DILL/TutorialTest.lean`.
